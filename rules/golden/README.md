## What this is, in plain terms

Guandan (掼蛋) is a four-player card game played with two decks. Players take turns putting down **plays**: a single card, a pair, a run of five, and so on. Each player must either beat the play on the table with a bigger play of the same kind, play a **bomb** (which beats ordinary plays), or pass.

`base-v1.md` writes those rules down, with a source quote for each one. This folder turns the rules into **concrete examples with answers**. For example: "at level 2, a seven plus the heart 2 makes a pair of sevens, and that pair beats a pair of sixes." When the rules engine is built, its tests can load these cases and check that the engine gives the same answers.

A few game terms the cases use:

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Level (级牌) | Each deal has a level rank, from 2 to A. Cards of that rank are **level cards**. They rank above A, but only when compared one against another. Inside a run they sit at their normal place. |
| Wildcard (逢人配) | The two **heart** level cards, for example both heart 2s at level 2. A wildcard can stand for any card except a joker. |
| Declaration | What the player says each wildcard stands for. In this app a play with a wildcard always carries one (decision D6). |
| Bomb | Four or more cards of one rank. It beats any ordinary play. Bigger bombs beat smaller ones. |
| Straight flush | Five cards in a row, all one suit. It ranks between the 5-card and 6-card bombs. |
| Four jokers | Both big and both small jokers. It beats everything. |

## How to read a case

```json
{
  "id": "WC01",
  "kind": "play",
  "status": "sourced",
  "decisions": [],
  "rules": ["R7.2", "R7.4", "R2.2", "R2.13"],
  "setup": {
    "level": "2",
    "wildcards_held": ["H2"],
    "hand": null,
    "table": {"cards": ["S6", "D6"], "declaration": null, "classification": {"type": "pair", "rank": "6"}},
    "play": {"cards": ["S7", "H2"], "declaration": [{"card": "H2", "as_rank": "7"}]}
  },
  "expected": {"legal": true, "classification": {"type": "pair", "rank": "7"}, "exclusive": false,
               "bomb_size": null, "straight_flush": false, "beats_table": true, "rejected_reason": null},
  "why": "..."
}
```

**Card notation:**

- A card is its suit letter (`S` spades, `H` hearts, `C` clubs, `D` diamonds) followed by its rank: `2`–`10`, `J`, `Q`, `K`, `A`.
- `LJ` is the small joker and `BJ` the big joker.
- Each exact card exists twice in the deal, so a case may list the same card twice. Every case's cards fit inside one real 108-card deal.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `kind` | `play` judges one play. `generator` says what the legal-move generator must offer for a hand. `profile` checks the rule profile. |
| `setup.table` | The play currently on the table. `null` means the play is a lead. |
| `play.declaration` | What each wildcard stands for (`as_rank`, plus `as_suit` only for a straight flush). `null` means none was given. |
| `expected.legal` | For a lead: the cards form a valid play under the given declaration. For a follow: the play is valid **and** beats the table. |
| `expected.classification` | The reading being judged. Sequences list their ranks low to high (`["A","2","3","4","5"]`). Other types give one `rank`. `null` means no valid reading. |
| `expected.exclusive` | `true` means this is the **only** legal reading of these cards. `false` means the case makes no claim about other readings. |
| `expected.beats_table` | `null` for a lead, or when the play has no valid reading. |
| `expected.rejected_reason` | A suggested label for why the play is refused. The rules do not define these names; the implementation may choose its own. |
| `why` | The hand derivation, citing the rule rows. |

## Unresolved items

These cases have **no expected result**, because no rule row in `base-v1.md` answers them. Each one needs a decision before it can get one.

| ID | Question | Needs |
|---|---|---|
| UN01 | Does a wildcard played as itself (alone, or as its own level rank) still need a declaration, and how is it written? R7.8 says every wildcard play carries a declaration. R7.9 says a lone wildcard counts as its natural self and "is not declared as another rank". Neither says what the declaration says. The golden set assumes it is written as `as_rank` = the level rank. Cases BS08, BR10, SQ20, WC19, WC21, WC22, WC24 and WC25 use that assumption. Their game results would not change under another format, but their inputs would. | Owner, as an extension of D6 |
| UN02 | A declaration that names a suit for a play that is not a straight flush (for example 666 + heart 2 declared as **club** 6). R7.10 says a suit is named only for straight flushes. It does not say whether an extra suit is rejected or ignored. | Owner, as an extension of D6 |

## Other findings

- **A 10-card bomb can never meet a 9-card bomb in the same deal.** Each needs at least one wildcard, the 10-card bomb needs both, and there are only two. So BR13 tests 10 against 8, and BR12 tests 9 against 9 (one wildcard each).
- **Some rules rest only on the reposted national texts.** 42 cases cite at least one rule whose quoted evidence comes only from the HA17 or CG22a reposts, with no Jiangsu 2017 or WG25 quote among that rule's evidence. If the printed national edition differs (decision D11), re-check those cases first. The audit lists them.
- **One kind of case was easy to mislabel.** Several cases give a wildcard declaration that makes no legal play, while the same cards have another legal reading: SQ20, WC16, WC17. Whether such a play is rejected, or quietly re-read, is the D6 product rule. So these cases are default-dependent, even when the declared reading itself is plainly illegal.

## What the audit does and does not show

`golden-v1.audit.md` re-read every cited rule row and source quote. It recomputed every expected value with an evaluator written separately from the cases, checked that no two cases contradict, and planted ten deliberate errors to confirm that it catches them. The final pass has 0 FAIL.

That shows the cases follow from the draft as one author reads it. It does not show that the draft is correct Guandan. The cases, the evaluator and the audit share one author. **A rules reviewer still needs to check both `base-v1.md` and these cases.**
